Abstract

Financial vocational education faces a persistent tension: classrooms are expected to produce graduates who can interpret business data, judge financial risk, and use intelligent tools, yet students enter with widely different accounting and programming backgrounds. This paper reports the design and pilot implementation of a teacher-led, student-centered precision teaching model supported by multimodal agents in a financial data analytics course. The model organizes teaching into three connected stages: pre-class precise diagnosis, in-class precise instruction, and post-class precise improvement. A group of pedagogical agents collects multimodal learning evidence, builds student profiles, generates differentiated tasks, provides practice support, and produces process-oriented feedback, while teachers retain instructional decisions and final evaluation. The implementation uses a real project, the development of an enterprise financial risk warning agent, as the course carrier. The paper describes the design principles, course transformation, agent roles, collaboration boundaries, and evaluation framework. Preliminary classroom observations suggest that the model is workable in ordinary vocational classrooms and that its value depends on clear task boundaries, teacher control, data compliance, and continuous revision of course resources.
